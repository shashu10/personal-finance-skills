# Research modes

Choose only modes that answer the user's question. A useful evidence table has:
claim, value/units, financial period, source and publication date, and uncertainty.
Keep unavailable facts visible rather than filling gaps from similarly named issuers.

## Business and financials

Read the latest annual and interim reports and material subsequent disclosures.
Use the applicable regulator and exchange for the listing; SEC filings are not
universal, and a foreign issuer's form may differ from a domestic company's form.
Review segment economics, customer concentration, pricing, competition, and dilution.
Trace cash conversion, capital expenditure, working capital, debt maturities and
liquidity; earnings alone do not establish solvency or distributable cash.

Separate a company's stated strategic position from independently supported evidence.
Treat supply-chain market-share claims and technology roadmaps as dated assertions.
Compare peers on consistent accounting periods, currency, share classes, and business
mix. Avoid carrying sector-specific rules into unrelated industries.

## Valuation and scenarios

Reconcile price, share count, market capitalization, cash, debt, and enterprise value
at stated dates. Distinguish basic, diluted, and fully diluted share counts, including
options, convertibles, and planned issuance where relevant. State which metric or
cash-flow definition a multiple uses. Show bear/base/bull drivers rather than an
unexplained target price. These are scenarios, not calibrated probabilities unless
there is evidence for the probability model.

Never infer personal taxable gains from market value minus a guessed cost basis.
Research need not assume selling anything. A later portfolio review can use confirmed
lots, jurisdictional research, user limits, and existing decisions.

## Technical and options analysis

Verify symbol/venue, timezone, interval, date coverage, split/dividend adjustment,
and missing observations. Use actual historical bars; do not draw conclusions from
synthetic examples. Explain the indicator's inputs and role in the thesis.

For options, report expiry, strike, bid/ask, quote time, liquidity, implied-volatility
definition, and spread. Last traded prices can be stale. A straddle-derived implied
move is a pricing heuristic, not a promise or a probability interval without a model.
Do not generalize one expiry's put/call ratio into a complete investor sentiment claim.

## IPO and other offerings

Read the latest registration statement/prospectus and amendments, plus the final
pricing notice when available. Label preliminary ranges as preliminary. Verify:

- Issuer and security class, offering/listing dates, exchange and symbol.
- Primary versus secondary shares, use of proceeds, fees, and selling holders.
- Pre/post-offering share counts, dilution, options/convertibles, and greenshoe.
- Voting/control rights, related-party transactions, customer and funding concentration.
- Revenue quality, losses/cash burn, liquidity runway, and auditor disclosures.
- Actual lockup provisions, expiry dates, exceptions, and potential sale supply.
- Allocation eligibility and settlement restrictions only if relevant to the user.

Compare enterprise value and operating metrics on the same post-offering basis.
Private secondary transaction prices are not necessarily the same security or terms.
A waitlist or requested allocation is not a fill. Before trading begins, technical
analysis can cover peers or a future plan, never nonexistent issuer price history.

## Report structure

Scale detail to the question. A concise decision memo can use: conclusion and
conditions; supporting and opposing evidence; valuation/scenarios; catalysts and
failure conditions; sources and missing data. Keep agent output labeled by engine
and run date. Multiple agreeing agents can share the same mistaken source.
