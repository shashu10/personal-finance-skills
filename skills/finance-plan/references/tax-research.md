# Tax and jurisdiction research

Gather facts and research applicable rules; do not derive universal tax conclusions
from a user's nationality, employer, or relocation goal.

## Minimum facts depend on the question

Establish the tax year, jurisdictions involved, relevant actual dates, and the type
and source of income or gain. Only ask about citizenship, residence permits,
presence history, filing status, ownership, household, treaties, or tax elections
when they affect the question. Reuse confirmed answers and do not guess missing ones.

A planned move is distinct from completed travel. Immigration status, tax residence,
domicile, payroll location, and physical work location are different facts. Account
location and asset listing venue do not alone determine the tax treatment.

## Source and qualify the conclusion

Consult current official tax-authority guidance, statutory material and relevant
treaty text for the tax year in question. Record effective dates, thresholds, conditions,
exceptions and filing obligations relevant to the question. Search results and
summaries are leads, not substitutes for the supporting source.

Separate the following in the output:

1. Confirmed personal facts and their evidence.
2. The applicable rule and exact primary-source link.
3. Your interpretation, assumptions, and unresolved classification questions.
4. Scenario arithmetic conditional on that interpretation.

Do not turn uncertain residence status into an assumed zero capital-gains rate.
Distinguish capital gains, dividends, interest, employment income, pension payments,
withholding, social contributions, wealth/estate taxes and reporting requirements.
The absence of one tax does not eliminate all of the others.

For lot-based calculations establish actual quantity, acquisition date, adjusted
basis, currency conversion method, holding-period rules, and corporate actions.
Broker-reported performance P/L is not necessarily tax P/L. Prior-year losses require
verified carryforward records and the jurisdiction's netting/usage rules.
Research wash-sale or similar anti-avoidance provisions when they actually apply.

## Material uncertainty

If missing facts materially change the result, show the alternative outcomes and
the specific evidence needed. Identify a specific question for professional review
when legal classification or filing choices remain unresolved; avoid a generic disclaimer that
obscures useful work. Do not submit filings, elections, or account certifications
as part of a planning calculation.

Persist source-backed research in a dated private report. A user confirming that
they received advice from a professional is a personal fact with provenance; it
does not make the underlying interpretation timeless or universally applicable.
